Workplace Wellness

Simple practices for more ease, energy & presence throughout your workday.

A quick reference by Melissa Rose

Scroll to begin

You don’t need to completely change your routine to support your well‑being. Small, intentional moments throughout the day can make a meaningful difference in how your body feels, how you manage stress, and how you show up for your work.

The Invitation

Choose 1–2 practices that work best for you — and practice often.

Pro Tip

Stick a fluorescent Post‑it note somewhere visible on your desk — like your screen — to remind you to practice.

No. 1

Reset Your Posture

There is no single “perfect” posture — your best posture is often your next posture. Throughout the day, check in:

  • Ground your energy — feel the soles of your feet. Use a small stool or footrest if needed to support your feet and ease tension through the hips and lower back.
  • Relax your toes. Notice if you’re gripping or curling them, and let them soften into the floor.
  • Soften your shoulders away from your ears.
  • Stack your head over your shoulders. Let your spine lengthen rather than reaching your head toward the screen.
  • Who is the king or queen? 👑 Bring your screen up to eye level rather than bowing your head to your screen.
  • Unclench your jaw. Soften the muscles around your mouth and lips.
  • Breathe. Notice when you’re holding your breath during tasks — and let it flow again.
  • Bring your keyboard and mouse toward you. Avoid constantly reaching forward.
  • Change positions often. Stand, shift, stretch, and move throughout your day.

No. 2

Take a Movement Snack

Our bodies are designed to move. A “movement snack” is simply 30–90 seconds of intentional movement woven throughout your day. You don’t have to wait for a workout to give your body what it needs.

Shoulder Rolls

Slowly roll your shoulders forward 5–10 times, then reverse the direction and roll them back 5–10 times.

Neck Release

Bring your left ear toward your left shoulder. Inhale as you gently lift your chin toward the sky, exhale as you lower your chin toward your chest. Move slowly between the two for a few breaths, then repeat on the right side.

Wrist + Forearm Stretch

Palms on the desk, fingertips pointing back toward you — gently lean back until you feel a stretch through the underside of your wrists and inner forearms.

Then flip your hands over, backs of the hands on the desk, fingertips toward you, and feel the stretch across the tops of your wrists.

3 slow breaths each

Seated Spinal Twist

Sit tall with both feet grounded. Inhale to lengthen your spine; exhale and gently rotate your torso to one side, using the chair for light support rather than pulling deeper. Return to center and repeat on the other side.

1–3 slow breaths per side

Chest + Heart Opener

Knees slightly wider than your hips, feet planted. Hold the back of your chair behind you and gently lean your chest forward.

Inhale: lift your chin, broaden the chest, gently arch. Exhale: lower your chin, gently round your spine.

Repeat for 3–5 breaths

Standing Side‑Body Stretch

Arms overhead: stand tall, reach both arms up, and gently stretch to each side.

Hands on hips: shift your hips to one side as your upper body moves the other way, creating length through the side of your body. Keep it comfortable and spacious — no need to push deeper.

1–3 slow breaths per side

Squats or Calf Raises

Stand up and do 10 slow squats or 10 calf raises to wake up the lower body and get things moving.

Walk It Out · Shake It Out

Take a quick walk around the office, walk to refill your water, or take the longer route.

Or stand up and bounce, wiggle, and shake your whole body for 10–30 seconds. Shake off unnecessary tension and reset.

🤰 A Special Note for Pregnant Bodies

During pregnancy, prolonged sitting can become especially uncomfortable. Make it a priority to stand, walk, change positions, or gently move at least once every 60 minutes.

Movement doesn’t have to mean a workout. Stand. Stretch. Shake. Walk. Breathe. Small bursts of movement woven throughout your day count.

No. 3

Use Your Breath as a Reset

Your breath is one of the simplest tools you have available to help shift your state.

The Physiological Sigh
Inhale through your nose→ take a second small inhale at the top→ release with a long, relaxed “haaaa” exhale

Repeat 1–3 times.

Before an important meeting After a stressful conversation Before a difficult email When overwhelm rises Between tasks

Or simply lengthen your exhale — a comfortable inhale, an exhale slightly longer, without forcing it. Sometimes a reset can be that simple.

No. 4

Create Mindful Transitions

It’s easy to carry the energy of one meeting, email, or interaction directly into the next. Instead, create a tiny pause:

Close task→ breathe→ notice & soften tension→ begin again

Before moving into your next task, take one intentional breath, relax somewhere you’re holding unnecessary tension, and ask yourself:

“How do I want to show up for what’s next?”

Even 15–30 seconds can create a meaningful transition.

No. 5

Give Your Eyes a Break

Hours of close‑up screen work can create visual fatigue. Remember the 20–20–20 practice:

20Every 20 minutes
20Look 20 feet away
20For 20 seconds
  • Look out a window into the distance.
  • Intentionally blink several times.
  • Alternate your focus between something near and something far.
  • Let your eyes completely leave your screen whenever you stand up.
No. 6

Build Wellness Into What You Already Do

The practices we’re most likely to maintain are the ones that don’t require us to completely rearrange our lives. Create simple cues:

Finish a meeting, email, or call→Take a breath
Sip your coffee→Sip your water
Notice tension→Change your posture
Stand up→Stretch, shake, or bounce for 30 seconds
Finish a task→Look away from your screen
Move into something new→Pause & intentionally transition

Small practices, repeated consistently, become part of your habits.

Your Micro‑Practice

When you only have a minute, this is how you come back to yourself.

  • Ground

    Feel the soles of your feet and the support beneath you.

  • Soften

    Unclench your jaw. Relax your shoulders. Feel the palms of your hands.

  • Breathe

    Take an intentional inhale and a slow, long exhale.

  • Notice

    Where are you tightening, or holding tension in your body right now?

  • Choose

    What would support you in this next moment to become present & available?

Take One Thing With You

You don’t need to implement everything at once. Choose one or two practices your body responded to and experiment with incorporating them into your workday.

Small moments of awareness, practiced consistently, can change the way we experience our entire day. Start small. Stay curious. Keep coming back to what feels good.
